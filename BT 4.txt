# Survey Report on Types of Blockchains and Their Real-Time Use Cases

## 1. Introduction

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that stores information in blocks connected using cryptographic techniques. It provides transparency, security, decentralization, and immutability. Blockchain allows multiple participants to share and verify information without depending entirely on a central authority.

Blockchain technology is widely used in banking, healthcare, supply chain management, digital identity, cryptocurrency, and government services.

## 2. Objectives

- To study different types of blockchain networks.
- To understand the working principles of blockchain.
- To explore real-world applications of blockchain technology.
- To compare different blockchain types.
- To understand the advantages and limitations of blockchain.

## 3. Types of Blockchains

### A. Public Blockchain

A public blockchain is an open and decentralized network where anyone can participate, view transactions, and validate transactions according to network rules.

**Features:**
- Open to everyone.
- Highly decentralized.
- Transparent transactions.
- Uses consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work and Proof of Stake.

**Examples:** Bitcoin and Ethereum.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**
- Cryptocurrency transactions.
- Decentralized Finance (DeFi).
- Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs).
- Smart contracts and decentralized applications.

### B. Private Blockchain

A private blockchain is a permissioned network controlled by a single organization. Only authorized participants can access the network and perform transactions.

**Features:**
- Restricted access.
- High transaction speed.
- Controlled by a central organization.
- Provides privacy and access control.

**Examples:** Hyperledger Fabric and private Ethereum networks.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**
- Internal banking operations.
- Company record management.
- Supply chain tracking.
- Secure sharing of business information.

### C. Consortium Blockchain

A consortium blockchain is managed by multiple organizations instead of a single authority. Only selected organizations can participate in the network.

**Features:**
- Controlled by multiple organizations.
- Permission-based access.
- Improved collaboration.
- Efficient transaction processing.

**Examples:** R3 Corda and consortium-based Hyperledger Fabric networks.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**
- Interbank transactions.
- Insurance claim processing.
- Supply chain management.
- Trade finance between organizations.

### D. Hybrid Blockchain

A hybrid blockchain combines features of public and private blockchains. It allows organizations to keep confidential information private while making selected data publicly accessible.

**Features:**
- Combines public and private access.
- Provides data privacy.
- Offers controlled transparency.
- Supports flexible access permissions.

**Examples:** Dragonchain and hybrid blockchain implementations using public and private networks.

**Real-Time Use Cases:**
- Healthcare record management.
- Government record systems.
- Real estate management.
- Enterprise supply chain applications.

## 4. Comparison of Blockchain Types

| Feature | Public | Private | Consortium | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Access | Open | Restricted | Selected members | Mixed |
| Control | Decentralized | Single organization | Multiple organizations | Combined |
| Transparency | High | Limited | Partial | Configurable |
| Speed | Network-dependent | Generally high | Generally high | Design-dependent |
| Privacy | Generally public | High | Controlled | Configurable |
| Example | Ethereum | Hyperledger Fabric | R3 Corda | Dragonchain |

## 5. Real-Time Applications of Blockchain

1. **Banking and Finance:** Blockchain enables secure transactions, cross-border payments, digital currencies, and decentralized finance.

2. **Healthcare:** It helps maintain secure medical records and allows authorized healthcare providers to share patient information.

3. **Supply Chain Management:** Blockchain tracks products from manufacturers to customers, improving transparency and reducing fraud.

4. **Digital Identity:** It supports secure digital identity verification and helps prevent identity theft.

5. **Real Estate:** Blockchain can maintain property ownership records and simplify property transactions.

6. **Education:** Educational institutions can issue and verify digital certificates using blockchain.

7. **Voting Systems:** Blockchain can provide transparent and tamper-resistant election records.

8. **Insurance:** It can automate claim processing and payments through smart contracts.

## 6. Advantages of Blockchain

- Decentralization reduces dependence on a single authority.
- Cryptography improves data security.
- Transparency helps participants verify transactions.
- Immutability makes recorded data difficult to alter.
- Smart contracts automate business processes.
- Distributed records improve traceability.

## 7. Limitations of Blockchain

- Some networks have scalability limitations.
- Transaction fees may be high.
- Certain consensus mechanisms consume significant energy.
- Implementation and maintenance can be complex.
- Regulatory and privacy challenges exist.
- Lost private keys can result in loss of access to digital assets.

## 8. Future Scope

Blockchain technology has significant potential in digital banking, decentralized applications, smart cities, healthcare, digital identity, and the Internet of Things (IoT). Integration with artificial intelligence and cloud computing may create more efficient and secure digital systems.

## 9. Conclusion

Blockchain is an innovative technology that provides secure, transparent, and decentralized data management. Public, private, consortium, and hybrid blockchains serve different purposes depending on access requirements, privacy, and control. Their applications in finance, healthcare, supply chains, education, and government demonstrate their importance in modern digital systems.
